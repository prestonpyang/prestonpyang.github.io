3D-Printed Compressed-Air Engine

Designed and iterated a compact single-cylinder air engine through two completed prototypes, moving from a binding first prototype to a smoother, bearing-supported assembly with fewer failure points, with a third revision in development.

Outcome

A single-cylinder air engine built to practice crank kinematics and design for FDM, inspired by Tom Stanton. Two revisions printed and running on compressed air; V2 added a 0.127 mm diametral clearance allowance, metal hardware, and bearings, and turns noticeably smoother than V1. V3 is in progress to close out rod-boss rigidity and O-ring sealing.

My contribution
  • Designed the crank mechanism and packaging in CAD
  • Printed, assembled, and ran two full revisions
  • Diagnosed each failure mode and specified the fix for the next revision
  • Selected hardware and bearings to replace printed pins and shafts
Objective & Constraints
Kinematics
Single-cylinder crank mechanism that turns continuously on compressed air without binding through the stroke
Design for FDM
Fits specified with numeric allowances rather than nominal CAD dimensions, since printed features drift
Friction & wear
Rotating interfaces carried on bearings and metal hardware instead of printed pins and shafts
Sealing
Piston O-ring seals consistently across the stroke, still open, targeted in V3

Engineering Decision

Because press-in printed shafts introduced friction and inconsistent tolerances, I moved those interfaces to bolted metal hardware and bearings in V2. This reduced binding and made assembly repeatable, isolating piston-rod wall thickness and O-ring sealing as the remaining issues for V3.

Reflection
  • Printed fits need numeric allowances. FDM drift showed up as binding, not as a dimension I could measure in CAD.
  • Do not print parts that need concentricity or rigidity.
  • Wall thickness at a press fit has to be sized for the press load. The rod boss deforms because it was not.
  • O-ring groove geometry comes from a datasheet. I guessed the V2 groove and it leaks, so V3 specs depth, width, and squeeze.
