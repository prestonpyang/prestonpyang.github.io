BAJA SAE Rim Optimization

Redesigned the Baja SAE aluminum wheel geometry to cut unsprung mass and rotational inertia while holding structural reliability, validated with FEA and carried through to a repeatable machining process.

Role
Design & Manufacturing Lead
Timeline
One competition season
Tools
CAD, FEA, CAM, CNC Milling
Status
Manufactured & installed
Finished wheels mounted on the car
Outcome

The redesigned wheel removed 204 g per wheel, 6.2% of its mass, while holding a minimum FEA safety factor of 1.22 with no new stress concentrations in the pocketed regions. Four CAD iterations, comparative FEA, and a purpose-built fixture took it from hand sketches to a machined wheel set now on the car.

My contribution
  • Mapped predicted load paths and candidate pocket patterns by hand
  • Iterated four CAD revisions, tracking mass and moment of inertia per version
  • Ran comparative FEA with loads, constraints, and mesh held constant
  • Designed the machining fixture, including anti-rotation locating
  • Programmed CAM and machined the wheel set in-house
Objective & Constraints

Fixture design & CAM strategy
Fixture iterations and clearance stack-up. A rotationally symmetric part needed anti-rotation locating, not just a re-findable WCS.

Because the wheel is rotationally symmetric, the biggest manufacturing risk was losing a consistent reference during clamping and cutting. I designed for a re-findable work coordinate system for rework and future runs, and for anti-rotation constraint at the fixture.

2D pocket toolpath. Fillet radii were matched to the end mill so each pocket finishes in a single pass.

CAM strategy focused on avoiding chatter caused by longer than expected tool engagement, which was needed for work clearance around the pockets.

Machining
Setup, cut, and finished wheel. The fixture held location across all four wheels without re-indicating.
Engineering Decision

Because the mass and MOI trade study showed diminishing return on further pocketing while FEA showed stress margin tightening near the deepest cuts, I selected the geometry that captured most of the mass reduction while keeping a safety factor of 1.22 to 1.26, rather than pushing to the most aggressive concept.

Validation

The selected geometry was verified through comparative FEA across all versions with matched loads, constraints, and mesh, rather than a single final check: no new stress concentration developed in the pocketed regions, no adverse load-path shift from removing mass, and a minimum safety factor of 1.22 to 1.26 under predicted bump, impact, and cornering loads, a tighter margin than typical for a rotating structural part, which is why further mass removal wasn't pursued. Physical validation came from a full season of competition running on the machined wheels with no reported failures.

Reflection
  • Tracking mass and MOI per version kept me from pocketing areas that looked aggressive but saved nothing.
  • I stopped at a 1.22 safety factor. More mass could have come out geometrically, but not with enough structural margin left on a rotating part.
  • Matching fillet radii to the end-mill radius in CAD avoided a machining problem later.
  • A rotationally symmetric part needs an anti-rotation feature in the fixture. I found that during fixture design, not before it.
